# Compliance: recording, accommodations, federal law and records

Last reviewed October 2026. Not legal advice.

## Recording and AI note-takers
- Recording or AI note-taking needs consent from everyone on the call. California, Illinois, Florida and other states require all-party consent, so get it every time: in the invite and at the start of the call. Turn it off if a candidate declines, with no penalty.

## Accommodations
- Offer accommodations in every invite and exercise prompt (extra time, captions, a different format, breaks).
- Start a good-faith conversation promptly and document the outcome. NYC requires a cooperative dialogue and a written final decision. People leadership decides, not interviewers.
- Using the same questions for everyone never justifies denying an accommodation.

## Federal anti-discrimination
- Title VII, the ADA, the ADEA, GINA and the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act apply to hiring.
- Federal agencies deprioritized disparate-impact enforcement in 2025-2026, but candidates can still bring claims in court and state laws still apply. Structured, job-related, consistent interviews remain the best protection.

## Records
- Keep interview notes, scorecards and exercises at least 1 year from the decision (federal minimum), longer where state law requires (for example, 4 years in California) or if a charge is filed.
- Notes record job-related evidence only. Never protected characteristics, guesses about them, or comments on appearance, accent or age.
