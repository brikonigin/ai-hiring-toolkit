# Interview standards: scoring, values and equity

Values and the decision rule come from `company-config.md`. The debrief discusses only competencies where interviewers disagree.

## Scoring scale

| Score | Meaning | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 4 - Strong Hire | Exceeds the bar | Specific, first-hand examples with results |
| 3 - Hire | Meets the bar | Solid examples with minor gaps |
| 2 - No Hire | Below the bar | Vague or secondhand examples, or needed heavy prompting |
| 1 - Strong No Hire | Well below the bar, or a red flag | No relevant evidence, or behavior that conflicts with the values |

Missing evidence is not a low score. If a question wasn't asked or answered, mark it "not assessed" and plan a follow-up.

Score behaviors, never "culture fit," similarity to the interviewer, shared employers or mutual connections.

## Equity checks
- Require competencies and evidence, not years of experience or degrees, unless a law or license requires them.
- Every scored competency has written 1-4 anchors; every question has "Look for" and "Red flag" guidance.
- Turn each failure mode the hiring manager describes into a question, red flag and anchor. Never name a past employee.
- State ramp expectations in the screen so candidates can opt out early.
- At 90 days after a hire, compare ramp results with interview scores and revise or drop questions that didn't predict performance.

## Candidate experience
- Every candidate gets warmth, clear timelines and a decision, including rejections.
- Candidate-facing language is warm, specific and plain. No "rockstar," "ninja," "work hard, play hard" or "hustle" language.
