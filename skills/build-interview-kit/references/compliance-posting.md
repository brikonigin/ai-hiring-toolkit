# Compliance: job posts, pay ranges and AI tools

Last reviewed October 2026. Not legal advice. Apply the rules for every location in scope; if unknown, apply the strictest rule and mark `[Location to confirm]`.

## Pay transparency
- Post a good-faith pay range on every posting covered by a pay transparency law, including NYC, New York State, California, Illinois and a growing list of other states. For remote roles, assume the posting is covered.
- Share the range in the recruiter screen, before the candidate invests time in the loop.

## AI and automated tools in hiring
- **NYC Local Law 144:** tools that score, rank or screen NYC candidates need an independent bias audit within 12 months, a public results summary, and notice at least 10 business days before use, with a way to request an alternative process or accommodation.
- **Illinois (HB 3773, effective 1/1/2026):** advance notice when AI is used in hiring; AI may not have a discriminatory effect. The AI Video Interview Act requires consent before AI evaluates recorded video interviews.
- **California:** CCPA automated decision-making rules took effect 1/1/2026; anti-bias testing is key evidence under the state's civil-rights rules.
- **Colorado, Connecticut, Maryland:** check current obligations (Colorado SB 26-189; Connecticut SB 5 phasing in from 10/1/2026; Maryland consent before facial recognition in interviews).
- **Standard for this toolkit:** interviewers score candidates; AI never makes or recommends a hiring decision.

## Equal opportunity language on every post
- An equal opportunity statement, how to request accommodations, and, where AI is used, how the company uses AI in hiring and how candidates may use AI in their application.
